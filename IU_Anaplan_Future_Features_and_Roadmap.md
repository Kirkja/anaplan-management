## Purpose
Provide a visible planning view of potential future capabilities without allowing unfinished implementation requirements to disappear into a loosely defined “future” category.

This document contains no verified feature entries. It does not assert vendor commitments, funding, contractual scope, or IU approval.

## Single-source-of-truth rule
The Implementation Backlog and Deferral Register holds the canonical record for every item. Reuse its IU-ANAPLAN-NNNN ID. This document holds a concise roadmap index and prioritization discussion, not a second independent lifecycle status.

When a future idea is first raised, create its canonical record in the register, classify it as a new request or unverified, and reference it here. If it returns to implementation, retain the same ID.

## Eligibility and boundaries
| Situation | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Genuine new capability beyond the verified implementation baseline | Future candidate; evaluate business value and feasibility. |
| Original requirement with authorized IU deferral | Eligible for this index; keep original baseline classification and approval evidence visible. |
| Original requirement with only a Deloitte recommendation to defer | Keep under proposed deferral in the register; do not present it as an agreed future enhancement. |
| Scope disputed or evidence incomplete | Keep dispute visible; optional watchlist reference must say “unresolved,” not “approved future.” |
| Defect or unmet acceptance criterion | Track as such; assess impact before considering a release change. |
| Dependency on a possible Anaplan product change | Record separately from an IU/Deloitte delivery plan; do not assume availability or a vendor roadmap commitment. |

Scope changes should be evaluated and approved through authorized governance rather than inferred from a roadmap label. [S1]

### Horizon definitions
- Next candidate: sufficiently understood for consideration in the next planning/release decision; not a promise.
- Later candidate: valuable but blocked by priority, dependencies, or readiness.
- Explore: problem or solution needs discovery before a delivery decision.
- Unscheduled: retained for visibility without a release target.

### Commitment definitions
- Idea: captured, not evaluated.
- Under assessment: value, feasibility, and effort under review.
- Approved for planning: IU agrees to develop a delivery proposal; not necessarily funded.
- Committed: named delivery owner, approved scope, funding/resource confirmation as applicable, and documented target/approval evidence.

A horizon or priority alone does not constitute a commitment. Roadmaps communicate direction and prioritization; backlog refinement clarifies and ranks actionable work. [S2][S3]

## Prioritization discussion
Use a qualitative assessment initially; avoid numeric scores that imply unsupported precision.

| Criterion | Questions |
|---|---|
| Business value | What budgeting outcome improves, and how will IU measure it? |
| Risk and obligation | Does continued delay expose a material operational, control, or accessibility risk? |
| Reach | Which users, units, and processes benefit? |
| Urgency | What budget-cycle deadline or external dependency matters? |
| Readiness | Are requirements, source data, ownership, and acceptance criteria ready? |
| Effort and total ownership cost | What build, integration, support, and training work is required? |
| Dependency leverage | Does this unlock other important work? |

Document the tradeoff and decision-maker, not just the resulting priority. Priority frameworks weigh business impact against constraints; select a method jointly rather than treating a score as approval. [S4]

## Proposed review and promotion process
1. Review candidate value, dependencies, and age at the agreed cadence.
2. Revalidate business need with the IU sponsor.
3. Confirm estimates and ownership with the proposed delivery organization.
4. Obtain required IU scope, funding, and release approvals.
5. Record the decision and evidence in the canonical register.
6. Update the roadmap view to reflect the documented commitment.
7. Validate delivered capability against acceptance criteria before closure.

Do not delete declined or superseded ideas without a retained disposition and history in the canonical record. Do not represent a Deloitte proposal as an Anaplan product commitment, or vice versa.

## Executive review questions
- Which candidates are genuinely new, and which are approved deferrals from baseline scope?
- Which entries have no accountable owner, estimate, or next review date?
- Which have a horizon but no funding or delivery commitment?
- What is the continuing cost or risk of each deferred requirement?
- Are any unresolved implementation gaps being mislabeled as enhancements?
- What did IU approve since the previous review, and where is the evidence?
